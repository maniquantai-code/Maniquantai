Playing chess with a friend online used to mean creating accounts on the same platform, adding each other, and navigating a matchmaking system built for strangers. A shareable room code is faster and removes all of that friction.

## Setting up a game in under a minute

1. Go to [Play with Friends](/play/friends) and choose **Create Room**.
2. Pick a time control — untimed, blitz, rapid, or a custom clock — and your preferred color (or let it randomize).
3. Share the generated room code or link with your friend through any messaging app.
4. Once they join using the code, the game starts automatically for both players in real time.

No account creation, no email verification, no app download — the room exists for the duration of the game.

## What real-time multiplayer actually includes

- **Live move syncing** — moves appear on both boards instantly through a persistent connection, not by refreshing the page.
- **Synchronized chess clocks** — both players see the same running clock, and time updates reflect server-side timing to prevent disputes.
- **In-game chat** — quick preset messages (or full chat, depending on the room) so you can talk trash or offer a draw without leaving the board.
- **Draw offers and resignation** — handled through the interface itself, with clear confirmation so games don't end on a misclick.
- **Rematch requests** — start a new game with the same opponent and settings with one click once a game ends.

## Tips for a smooth multiplayer game

- **Agree on a time control before creating the room.** Untimed games can be relaxed and analytical; timed games test practical decision-making — pick based on what you both want out of the session.
- **Use a stable connection.** Real-time multiplayer relies on a persistent connection; switching networks mid-game (e.g. wifi to mobile data) can cause a brief reconnect delay.
- **Spectator links work well for teaching.** If you want a third person to watch and learn, most rooms support a spectator view without giving them the ability to move pieces.

## Multiplayer vs. playing the AI

Multiplayer and AI serve different purposes. Human opponents introduce psychological factors, unconventional plans, and genuine time pressure that AI at any difficulty doesn't fully replicate. AI, by contrast, is available instantly, at a consistent skill level, and is ideal for drilling specific openings or difficulty tiers without waiting for an opponent. ## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to create an account to play with a friend?**
No. Rooms are created and joined with a shareable code, with no account or email required.

**What happens if my friend disconnects mid-game?**
The game typically pauses briefly to allow reconnection; if the disconnect is prolonged, standard resignation/timeout rules apply depending on the room's settings.

**Can more than two people join a room?**
The two players occupy the White and Black seats; additional participants can typically join as spectators.
